Sins & Chakras: A Diagnostic Framework
The 7 Deadly Sins, 7 Virtues & Chakra Alignment — cross-system reference guide
Historical Grounding
The 7 deadly sins are not a Biblical list. They were systematized by the desert monk Evagrius Ponticus (4th c.) as logismoi — spiritual thought-patterns — refined by Gregory the Great and codified by Aquinas. This makes the chakra synthesis defensible: both systems map patterns of consciousness, not moral verdicts. The sins are diagnostic signals — not failures to condemn, but wounds to locate and move through.
Key Distinctions — Blocked vs. Underactive vs. Overactive
🧱 Blocked
Complete stagnation — energy can't move
The sin is latent. The root wound is present but not yet behaviorally expressed. Often hardest to detect — no outward performance, just frozen potential.
📉 Underactive
Insufficient flow
The sin turns inward — shame-based, collapsed, self-directed. Behaviorally passive; internally loud. The wound performs itself quietly against the self.
📈 Overactive
Excess, unregulated flow
The sin turns outward — compulsive, other-directed, dominating. Most externally visible; often mistaken for strength or virtue in toxic systems.

Deep Reference — Chakra by Chakra
🔴 Root · Muladhara
Survival · Grounding · Material Reality
Greed
Greed isn't fundamentally moral failure — it's scarcity consciousness lodged in the body.
🧱 Blocked
Full freeze. Scarcity wound calcified below behavior. Dissociation from physical reality; inability to receive even when safety exists.
📉 Underactive
Classic greed — accumulation from fear. Poverty mindset even in abundance. Inability to give or trust in "enough."
📈 Overactive
Territorial, acquisition-as-identity. Materialism as existential security. Hoarding control over environment and people.
🟠 Sacral · Svadhisthana
Desire · Creativity · Pleasure · Emotion
Lust
Religious shame most frequently lodges here. The Biblical weaponization of lust against queer bodies operates almost entirely through this center.
🧱 Blocked
Sexual, creative, and emotional shutdown. Anhedonia. Desire exists but is frozen — often through religious or relational trauma.
📉 Underactive
Desire turned inward — obsessive fantasy, shame, compulsive secrecy. Lust without the capacity to act, ask, or feel worthy.
📈 Overactive
Compulsive sexuality; using pleasure as control or escape. Inability to experience desire without consuming it.
🟡 Solar Plexus · Manipura
Will · Identity · Ego · Personal Power
WrathPride
Pride is ego inflation; Wrath is what happens when Pride is threatened. Aquinas named Pride (superbia) the root sin — tracking, since this is the ego's seat.
🧱 Blocked
Complete collapse of will. Victim identity. Shame functioning as paralysis rather than as information.
📉 Underactive
Internalized wrath (self-loathing, self-sabotage). Pride flipped inward as inferiority — performing smallness, deflecting.
📈 Overactive
Narcissistic rage, domination, arrogance. Wrath as power performance; Pride as social armor.
💚 Heart · Anahata
Love · Compassion · Connection · Grief
Envy
Envy is a heart deficiency problem — it emerges when the heart can't generate its own abundance, so it fixates on others who appear to have it.
🧱 Blocked
Grief that can't move. Complete emotional shutdown — neither envy nor love is accessible. The wound precedes both.
📉 Underactive
Coveting others' joy, relationships, or qualities because the heart can't generate its own. Envy as a love-deficit signal.
📈 Overactive
Love-as-control; possessiveness and jealousy framed as devotion. Merger rather than connection.
🔵 Throat · Vishuddha
Expression · Voice · Truth · Communication
Gluttony
Gluttony at its root is consumption filling the void of unexpressed truth. When needs can't be voiced, the body finds other ways to fill the gap.
🧱 Blocked
Complete inability to name or ask for needs. The vacuum fills with consumption — food, substances, media — as substitutes for expression.
📉 Underactive
Emotional eating, passive overconsumption, "eating feelings." Gluttony as a language problem — need unspoken becomes need swallowed.
📈 Overactive
Verbal excess, information hoarding, dominating conversation, gossip — consuming and producing in excess simultaneously.
🔮 Third Eye · Ajna
Perception · Discernment · Intuition · Inner Vision
SlothPride
Acedia in its original monastic sense wasn't laziness — it was spiritual torpor, a refusal to engage inner life. Maps directly to discernment failure.
🧱 Blocked
Willful blindness. Denial as a survival strategy. Complete inability to perceive spiritual reality or engage in discernment.
📉 Underactive
Magical thinking, delusion, outsourcing discernment to external authority. "I'll figure it out eventually" as spiritual avoidance.
📈 Overactive
Intellectual arrogance; weaponizing "insight" as superiority. Confusing intuition with ego — the critical risk for serious discernment practitioners.
⚪ Crown · Sahasrara
Divine Connection · Unity · Transcendence
Pride (Superbia)
Theologically: the sin of Lucifer — not moral failure but ontological rupture. Placing the self at the apex of divine hierarchy. The most subtle, and most dangerous, dysfunction.
🧱 Blocked
Nihilism, spiritual abandonment, feeling cut off from or unworthy of the divine. The wound beneath all the others.
📉 Underactive
Compulsive spiritual seeking (bypassing) without capacity to receive or integrate. Collecting enlightenment rather than embodying it.
📈 Overactive
Messianic thinking, spiritual ego, the belief one has "arrived" — in both frameworks, the surest sign one hasn't.

The Core Teaching

Blocked chakras hold the wound. Under- and overactive chakras are how that wound performs itself in behavior. The sins are not moral failures to condemn — they are diagnostic signals. Spiritual liberation begins not with self-judgment, but with location: where in the body does this pattern live, and what is it trying to protect?

Three Principles
01
Sins Are Signals
The logismoi were diagnostic patterns long before they were moral verdicts. They locate where energy is wounded — not evidence of a failed soul.
02
Location Before Liberation
Blocked chakras hold the wound silently. Under/overactive states are that wound's behavioral performance. Healing requires location first.
03
Shame Is the Wrong Tool
Liberation begins with discernment, not condemnation. This framework applies equally to Biblical study, somatic practice, and pastoral work.
